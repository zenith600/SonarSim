1. ACOUSTIC WAVE FUNDAMENTALS
================================================================================

WHAT IS SOUND IN WATER?
-----------------------
Sound is a pressure wave that propagates through water by compressing and
rarefying the medium. Unlike electromagnetic waves, sound REQUIRES a medium
to travel.

KEY PROPERTIES:
---------------
Frequency (f): Number of oscillations per second [Hz or kHz]
  - Human hearing: 20 Hz - 20 kHz
  - Sonar range: 30 kHz - 200 kHz (this simulator)
  - Higher frequency = better resolution, shorter range

Wavelength (λ): Physical length of one cycle [meters]
  Formula: λ = c/f
  Where c = sound speed (~1500 m/s in water)
  
  Examples at different frequencies:
  - 30 kHz:  λ = 1500/30000 = 0.05 m = 5 cm
  - 70 kHz:  λ = 1500/70000 = 0.021 m = 2.1 cm
  - 200 kHz: λ = 1500/200000 = 0.0075 m = 7.5 mm

Period (T): Time for one complete cycle [seconds]
  Formula: T = 1/f
  
  Examples:
  - 70 kHz: T = 1/70000 = 0.0000143 s = 14.3 microseconds

Amplitude (A): Strength of the pressure variation
  - Measured in Pascals (Pa) or dB re 1 μPa
  - Determines how "loud" the sound is
  - Energy proportional to A²

Phase (φ): Position within the oscillation cycle [radians, 0 to 2π]
  - Determines constructive/destructive interference
  - Changes with distance traveled: Δφ = 2π × (distance/λ)


SONAR EQUATION (Fundamental):
------------------------------
The passive sonar equation relates signal levels:

  SL - TL = RL

Where:
  SL = Source Level (transmitted power) [dB re 1 μPa @ 1m]
  TL = Transmission Loss (energy lost in propagation) [dB]
  RL = Received Level (signal at hydrophone) [dB re 1 μPa]

Example:
  - Source: 175 dB (typical chirp sonar)
  - After 50m with 1 reflection: TL ≈ 35 dB
  - Received: 175 - 35 = 140 dB


DECIBEL SCALE:
--------------
Why use dB? Because acoustic intensities span many orders of magnitude!

  dB = 20 × log₁₀(A/A_ref)     [for amplitude/pressure]
  dB = 10 × log₁₀(I/I_ref)     [for intensity/power]

Reference: 1 μPa (micro-Pascal) at 1 meter

Converting dB to linear amplitude factor:
  Amplitude_factor = 10^(dB/20)

Examples:
  +6 dB  = 2× amplitude (4× intensity)
  +20 dB = 10× amplitude (100× intensity)
  -20 dB = 0.1× amplitude (0.01× intensity)
  -40 dB = 0.01× amplitude (0.0001× intensity)


================================================================================
2. RAY THEORY AND APPROXIMATIONS
================================================================================

WHAT IS A RAY?
--------------
A ray is an idealized path representing the direction of energy flow.
Think of it as a thin beam of sound traveling in a straight line (until it
reflects or refracts).

WHEN IS RAY THEORY VALID?
--------------------------
Ray approximation works when:
  ✓ Wavelength << Object size
  ✓ Wavelength << Pool dimensions
  ✓ Frequency is high enough

Rule of thumb: Object size > 10 × wavelength

For 70 kHz (λ = 2.1 cm):
  ✓ Pool walls (meters): Ray theory perfect
  ✓ Gates (1-2 meters): Ray theory good
  ✓ Small fish (10 cm): Ray theory okay
  ✗ Plankton (1 mm): Ray theory breaks down (need wave theory)

WHY USE RAYS?
-------------
1. Computationally efficient (compared to solving wave equation)
2. Intuitive visualization (see sound paths)
3. Captures key phenomena: reflection, refraction, multipath
4. Good accuracy for sonar frequencies (30-200 kHz)

LIMITATIONS:
------------
✗ Doesn't model diffraction (bending around obstacles)
✗ Doesn't model interference patterns in detail
✗ Assumes high frequency (geometric optics regime)
✗ No edge effects or shadowing

3. SOUND SPEED IN WATER
================================================================================

WHY SOUND SPEED MATTERS:
------------------------
Sound speed (c) determines:
1. How fast acoustic signals travel
2. Ray bending (refraction) when c varies
3. Wavelength: λ = c/f
4. Time delays for multipath arrivals

TYPICAL VALUES:
---------------
Fresh water (20°C):    ~1481 m/s
Sea water (20°C):      ~1522 m/s
Warm ocean (30°C):     ~1545 m/s
Cold deep ocean (2°C): ~1450 m/s

FACTORS AFFECTING SOUND SPEED:
-------------------------------

1. TEMPERATURE (Dominant factor)
   Formula: Δc ≈ +4.6 m/s per °C increase
   
   Physical reason: Warmer molecules move faster, transmit pressure waves
   more quickly.
   
   Example:
   - At 10°C: c ≈ 1490 m/s
   - At 20°C: c ≈ 1536 m/s (+46 m/s)
   - At 30°C: c ≈ 1582 m/s (+92 m/s from 10°C)

2. PRESSURE/DEPTH (Important in deep water)
   Formula: Δc ≈ +0.016 m/s per meter depth
   
   Physical reason: Higher pressure compresses water, making it stiffer,
   transmitting sound faster.
   
   Example:
   - Surface (0m):  c₀
   - 50m depth:     c₀ + 0.8 m/s
   - 1000m depth:   c₀ + 16 m/s
   
   Note: For shallow pools (< 50m), depth effect is small.

3. SALINITY (Ocean vs fresh water)
   Formula: Δc ≈ +1.3 m/s per ppt (parts per thousand)
   
   Physical reason: Dissolved salts increase water density and stiffness.
   
   Example:
   - Fresh water (0 ppt):    c_base
   - Brackish (15 ppt):      c_base + 19.5 m/s
   - Ocean (35 ppt):         c_base + 45.5 m/s


MACKENZIE EQUATION (Complete Model):
-------------------------------------
The simulator uses the Mackenzie (1981) equation, which combines all effects:

c = 1448.96 + 4.591×T - 5.304×10⁻²×T² + 2.374×10⁻⁴×T³
    + 1.340×(S-35) + 1.630×10⁻²×D + 1.675×10⁻⁷×D²
    - 1.025×10⁻²×T×(S-35) - 7.139×10⁻¹³×T×D³

Where:
  T = Temperature (°C)
  S = Salinity (ppt)
  D = Depth (meters)
  c = Sound speed (m/s)

This is accurate to ±0.07 m/s for:
  - Temperature: 0-35°C
  - Salinity: 0-45 ppt
  - Depth: 0-8000 m

Default simulation values:
  T = 15°C (typical pool water)
  S = 0 ppt (fresh water)
  D = varies with position


SOUND SPEED PROFILE:
--------------------
In real oceans, sound speed varies with depth, creating a "sound speed profile."

Typical ocean profile:
  Surface layer:    Fast (warm, ~1520 m/s)
  Thermocline:      Decreasing (cooling)
  Deep water:       Increasing (pressure effect)
  
This creates a "sound channel" where rays can travel long distances.

In pools (this simulator):
  - Nearly constant temperature (well-mixed)
  - Slight increase with depth (pressure)
  - Very small gradients (< 1 m/s total change)

4. ENERGY LOSS MECHANISMS
================================================================================

Sound energy decreases as rays propagate. Four main mechanisms:

----------------------
4.1 GEOMETRIC SPREADING
----------------------

PHYSICAL PRINCIPLE:
As sound travels away from a point source, energy spreads over an increasingly
large area. Total energy is conserved, but energy per unit area decreases.

Think of it like inflating a balloon: the rubber stretches thinner as the
balloon expands.

SPHERICAL SPREADING (Omnidirectional source):
  
  Surface area of sphere: A = 4πr²
  Intensity: I = Power/Area = P/(4πr²) ∝ 1/r²
  Amplitude: A ∝ √I ∝ 1/r
  
  Transmission Loss: TL = 20 × log₁₀(r/r₀) dB
  
  Where:
    r = distance from source
    r₀ = reference distance (usually 1 meter)
  
  Examples:
  - 1m:   TL = 0 dB     (reference)
  - 10m:  TL = 20 dB    (amplitude × 0.1)
  - 100m: TL = 40 dB    (amplitude × 0.01)
  - 1km:  TL = 60 dB    (amplitude × 0.001)
  
  Doubling distance adds 6 dB loss!

CYLINDRICAL SPREADING (Waveguide/channel):
  
  In shallow water or ducts, sound spreads in 2D (not 3D):
  
  TL = 10 × log₁₀(r/r₀) dB
  
  Less loss than spherical! (Half as much in dB)
  
  Examples:
  - 1m:   TL = 0 dB
  - 10m:  TL = 10 dB    (vs 20 dB spherical)
  - 100m: TL = 20 dB    (vs 40 dB spherical)

SIMULATOR IMPLEMENTATION:
  Uses spherical spreading after 1m distance.
  In near field (< 1m), spreading loss is minimal.


--------------------------
4.2 ABSORPTION (ATTENUATION)
--------------------------

PHYSICAL PRINCIPLE:
Water molecules absorb acoustic energy and convert it to heat. This is a
TRUE energy loss (unlike spreading, which just redistributes energy).

Absorption is STRONGLY frequency-dependent: high frequencies absorbed much
faster than low frequencies.

PHYSICAL MECHANISMS:
1. Viscous absorption: Friction between water molecules
2. Chemical relaxation: Boric acid and magnesium sulfate in seawater
3. Molecular relaxation: Rotational/vibrational modes of water molecules

THORP EQUATION:
The simulator uses the Thorp equation (extended for high frequencies):

  α(f) = [0.11×f²/(1+f²)] + [44×f²/(4100+f²)] + 2.75×10⁻⁴×f² + 0.003

Where:
  α = absorption coefficient (dB/km)
  f = frequency (kHz)

FREQUENCY DEPENDENCE (Fresh water, approximate):

  Frequency    Absorption     Range*
  --------     ----------     ------
  10 kHz       0.1 dB/km      Very long
  30 kHz       0.9 dB/km      Long
  50 kHz       2.0 dB/km      Medium
  70 kHz       3.5 dB/km      Medium
  100 kHz      6.8 dB/km      Short
  150 kHz      14.5 dB/km     Short
  200 kHz      24.0 dB/km     Very short
  
  * Range = distance where absorption loss = 20 dB

RULE OF THUMB:
  Doubling frequency roughly doubles absorption!
  
  This is why:
  - Low frequency (10-50 kHz): Long-range sonar
  - High frequency (100-200 kHz): Short-range imaging

TRANSMISSION LOSS FROM ABSORPTION:

  TL_absorption = α(f) × distance_km

Examples for 70 kHz (α = 3.5 dB/km):
  - 10m (0.01 km):   TL = 0.035 dB  (negligible)
  - 100m (0.1 km):   TL = 0.35 dB   (small)
  - 1000m (1 km):    TL = 3.5 dB    (significant)
  - 10 km:           TL = 35 dB     (major loss)

AMPLITUDE FACTOR:
  Amplitude_factor = 10^(-TL/20)
  
  Example: 100m at 70 kHz
    TL = 0.35 dB
    Factor = 10^(-0.35/20) = 0.963
    Energy retained: 96.3%


--------------------
4.3 SCATTERING LOSS
--------------------

PHYSICAL PRINCIPLE:
Sound scatters off particles, bubbles, turbulence, and organisms in the water.
This redirects energy in random directions (away from the receiver).

TYPES OF SCATTERING:

1. RAYLEIGH SCATTERING (Small particles, λ >> particle size)
   - Scattering ∝ f⁴
   - Very frequency dependent
   - Minimal for low frequencies

2. MIE SCATTERING (Particles ~ wavelength)
   - Complex frequency dependence
   - Resonant effects

3. GEOMETRIC SCATTERING (Particles >> wavelength)
   - Frequency independent
   - Simple reflection off particles

SIMULATOR MODEL:
  β = 0.001 × (f/100)² dB/km
  
  This is a simplified Rayleigh-like model with f² dependence.

Examples:
  30 kHz:  β = 0.00009 dB/km  (negligible)
  70 kHz:  β = 0.00049 dB/km  (very small)
  200 kHz: β = 0.004 dB/km    (small)

NOTE: Scattering is typically MUCH smaller than absorption for the distances
and frequencies in this simulator. Included for completeness.


------------------------
4.4 BOUNDARY LOSSES
------------------------

PHYSICAL PRINCIPLE:
When sound hits a boundary (wall, seabed, surface, object), some energy is:
  - Reflected back into the water
  - Transmitted into the material (lost)
  - Converted to heat in the material

REFLECTION COEFFICIENT (R):
  Fraction of incident energy that is reflected.
  
  R = 0.0: Perfect absorber (anechoic chamber)
  R = 0.5: Half reflected, half absorbed
  R = 1.0: Perfect reflector (mirror)

MATERIAL PROPERTIES:

  Material              Reflection Coefficient (R)    Loss per bounce
  ----------------      -------------------------     ---------------
  Water-Air Surface     0.98 - 0.99                   1-2%
  Concrete (smooth)     0.85 - 0.95                   5-15%
  Metal (steel)         0.92 - 0.98                   2-8%
  Rock (hard)           0.80 - 0.90                   10-20%
  Sediment (sand)       0.40 - 0.70                   30-60%
  Mud (soft)            0.10 - 0.40                   60-90%
  
WHY SUCH VARIATION?
  - Density contrast: Bigger difference = more reflection
  - Rigidity: Stiffer materials reflect better
  - Surface roughness: Smooth reflects better
  - Porosity: Porous materials absorb energy

ACOUSTIC IMPEDANCE (Z):
  Z = ρ × c
  Where:
    ρ = density (kg/m³)
    c = sound speed (m/s)
  
  Reflection coefficient between two media:
    R = |(Z₂ - Z₁)/(Z₂ + Z₁)|²
  
  Examples:
  - Water: Z ≈ 1.5 × 10⁶ Pa·s/m
  - Air: Z ≈ 400 Pa·s/m  (huge mismatch! → R ≈ 0.9999)
  - Concrete: Z ≈ 7 × 10⁶ Pa·s/m (moderate match → R ≈ 0.8)


ANGLE-DEPENDENT REFLECTION:
----------------------------
Reflection loss depends on incidence angle!

  θ = 0° (Normal incidence): Perpendicular hit
  θ = 90° (Grazing incidence): Glancing blow

SMOOTH SURFACES:
  - Normal (0°): Maximum energy absorption
  - Grazing (>60°): Better reflection (less absorption)
  
ROUGH SURFACES:
  - Normal (0°): Good reflection (specular)
  - Grazing (>60°): Scattering (diffuse), less total reflection

SIMULATOR MODEL:
  
  Base reflection: R_base (material property)
  
  Roughness factor: R_rough = exp(-((θ/45°)²) × 0.1)
    Peaks at normal incidence (θ=0°)
  
  Effective reflection: R_eff = R_base × (0.7 + 0.3 × R_rough)
  
  Grazing penalty (θ > 60°):
    R_eff = R_eff × 0.9^((θ-60°)/10°)
  
Examples for concrete (R_base = 0.90):
  - 0° (normal):    R ≈ 0.90  (10% loss)
  - 30°:            R ≈ 0.88  (12% loss)
  - 60°:            R ≈ 0.86  (14% loss)
  - 80° (grazing):  R ≈ 0.74  (26% loss)

This means: MORE bounces at high angles = MORE energy loss!


CUMULATIVE EFFECT OF MULTIPLE BOUNCES:
---------------------------------------
Each reflection multiplies the amplitude by R.

After N bounces: Amplitude = A₀ × R^N

Examples for R = 0.90:
  1 bounce:  90% remaining
  2 bounces: 81% remaining
  5 bounces: 59% remaining
  10 bounces: 35% remaining
  15 bounces: 21% remaining
  20 bounces: 12% remaining

This is why rays terminate after many bounces!


================================================================================
5. REFLECTION PHYSICS
================================================================================

LAW OF REFLECTION:
------------------
"Angle of incidence = Angle of reflection"

Both angles measured from surface NORMAL (perpendicular).

Mathematical formula:
  r = d - 2(d·n)n
  
Where:
  d = incident ray direction (unit vector)
  n = surface normal (unit vector)
  r = reflected ray direction
  · = dot product

SPECULAR vs DIFFUSE REFLECTION:
--------------------------------

SPECULAR (Mirror-like):
  - Smooth surface (roughness << wavelength)
  - Well-defined reflection angle
  - Most energy in one direction
  - Example: Calm water surface, polished metal

DIFFUSE (Scattered):
  - Rough surface (roughness ~ wavelength)
  - Energy scattered in many directions
  - No single reflection angle
  - Example: Rough concrete, gravel seabed

TRANSITION CRITERION (Rayleigh Roughness):
  
  If surface roughness height (h) satisfies:
    h < λ/(8 × cos θ)
  Then: Specular reflection dominates
  
  Otherwise: Diffuse scattering increases

Example for 70 kHz (λ = 2.1 cm):
  At normal incidence (θ = 0°):
    h < 2.1/8 = 0.26 cm → Smooth if h < 2.6 mm
  
  At 60° grazing:
    h < 2.1/4 = 0.53 cm → Smooth if h < 5.3 mm

PHASE CHANGES:
--------------

HARD SURFACE (pressure-amplitude boundary):
  - Concrete, metal, rock
  - NO phase change on reflection
  - Pressure antinode at surface

SOFT SURFACE (pressure-release boundary):
  - Water-air interface
  - 180° phase shift (π radians)
  - Pressure node at surface
  - Reflects with inverted pressure

WHY IT MATTERS:
  Phase changes affect interference patterns when multiple rays arrive at
  a hydrophone. Rays with different phases can interfere constructively
  (add up) or destructively (cancel).

6. REFRACTION AND RAY BENDING
================================================================================

SNELL'S LAW:
------------
When sound passes through regions of different sound speed, rays bend.

  sin(θ₁)/c₁ = sin(θ₂)/c₂

Where:
  θ₁, θ₂ = angles from vertical
  c₁, c₂ = sound speeds in regions 1 and 2

WHICH WAY DO RAYS BEND?
  Rays bend TOWARD the region of SLOWER sound speed.
  
  If c₂ > c₁: Ray bends away from normal (away from vertical)
  If c₂ < c₁: Ray bends toward normal (toward vertical)

EXAMPLE: Temperature gradient
  - Surface water warm (fast sound): c = 1520 m/s
  - Deep water cold (slow sound): c = 1480 m/s
  
  Downward ray: Bends toward vertical (gets steeper)
  Upward ray: Bends away from vertical (gets flatter)

SOUND CHANNELS:
---------------
Special sound speed profiles can trap rays:

  Surface duct: Fast at surface, slow below → rays trapped near surface
  Deep channel: Minimum speed at mid-depth → rays trapped in channel
  
These allow VERY long-range propagation (hundreds of km in ocean).

SIMULATOR IMPLEMENTATION:
--------------------------
  The simulator computes sound speed gradient:
    ∇c = (∂c/∂x, ∂c/∂y, ∂c/∂z)
  
  Rays bend continuously based on local gradient.
  
  In typical pools: Gradients are SMALL (well-mixed water), so refraction
  is minimal compared to reflections. But it's included for accuracy.

7. PHASE AND INTERFERENCE
================================================================================

PHASE ACCUMULATION:
-------------------
As a ray travels, its phase advances:

  Δφ = 2π × (distance/λ) = 2π × f × (distance/c)

Example: 70 kHz ray traveling 10 meters
  λ = 1500/70000 = 0.0214 m
  Δφ = 2π × (10/0.0214) = 2π × 467 = 2933 radians
      = 467 complete cycles

PHASE TRACKING:
  The simulator tracks phase modulo 2π (keeps it in 0 to 2π range).
  
  Used for:
  - Determining arrival time
  - Calculating interference when multiple paths arrive

INTERFERENCE:
-------------
When two rays arrive at the same point:

CONSTRUCTIVE (phases aligned):
  φ₁ - φ₂ = 0, 2π, 4π, ... (even multiples of π)
  Amplitudes ADD: A_total = A₁ + A₂
  
DESTRUCTIVE (phases opposite):
  φ₁ - φ₂ = π, 3π, 5π, ... (odd multiples of π)
  Amplitudes SUBTRACT: A_total = A₁ - A₂
  Can cancel completely if A₁ = A₂!

PARTIAL (intermediate phase):
  A_total = √(A₁² + A₂² + 2×A₁×A₂×cos(φ₁-φ₂))

EXAMPLE: Two equal rays (A₁ = A₂ = 1.0)
  φ₁ - φ₂ = 0°:    A = 2.0  (4× intensity!)
  φ₁ - φ₂ = 90°:   A = 1.41 (2× intensity)
  φ₁ - φ₂ = 180°:  A = 0.0  (complete cancellation!)

MULTIPATH FADING:
-----------------
In real environments, small changes in position or frequency can change
the interference pattern dramatically. This causes signal fading.


================================================================================
8. MULTIPATH PROPAGATION
================================================================================

WHAT IS MULTIPATH?
------------------
Sound can reach a receiver via MULTIPLE PATHS:
  - Direct path (straight line)
  - Surface-reflected path
  - Bottom-reflected path
  - Multiple bounces off walls
  - Combinations of above

Each path has:
  - Different travel distance
  - Different arrival time
  - Different amplitude (different losses)
  - Different phase

EXAMPLE SCENARIO:
-----------------
Emitter at (10, 20, 2m), Hydrophone at (90, 180, 45m)

Possible paths:
  1. Direct: ~223m, ~148ms, high amplitude
  2. One bottom bounce: ~225m, ~150ms, medium amplitude
  3. One surface bounce: ~227m, ~151ms, medium amplitude
  4. Wall reflection: ~235m, ~157ms, lower amplitude
  5. Two bounces: ~245m, ~163ms, low amplitude

TIME SPREADING:
  Earliest arrival: 148ms
  Latest arrival: 163ms
  Spread: 15ms
  
This limits the pulse duration you can use! If pulse > 15ms, paths overlap.

PULSE DISPERSION:
-----------------
A short transmitted pulse becomes a LONG received signal due to multipath.

  Transmitted: Sharp ping (1ms)
  Received: Extended echo (10-20ms)
  
This is why chirp signals are useful: they can be compressed after reception.

ARRIVAL STRUCTURE:
------------------
Typical arrival pattern at hydrophone:

  Time →
  |-------|---------|-------------|------------------
  Direct  Surface   Bottom        Multiple bounces
  (strong)(medium)  (medium)      (weak, many)

MULTIPATH ADVANTAGE:
  More paths = more chances to detect signal
  Diversity improves detection in noise

MULTIPATH DISADVANTAGE:
  Echoes cause interference
  Limits communication rate
  Complicates signal processing


================================================================================
9. DETECTION AND HYDROPHONES
================================================================================

HYDROPHONE OPERATION:
---------------------
A hydrophone is an underwater microphone that converts pressure variations
into electrical signals.

DETECTION CRITERION:
  Hydrophone detects a ray if the ray passes within detection_radius.
  
  Typical: detection_radius = 0.5 to 2.0 meters

ARRIVAL INFORMATION RECORDED:
------------------------------
For each detected ray, the hydrophone records:

1. ARRIVAL TIME:
   t = path_length / c_avg
   
   Where c_avg is average sound speed along path.
   
   Precision: Microseconds to milliseconds
   
   Used for:
   - Ranging (distance = c × t)
   - Time-of-arrival (TOA) localization
   - Distinguishing multipath components

2. AMPLITUDE:
   Determines signal strength at receiver.
   
   Affected by:
   - Source power
   - Geometric spreading
   - Absorption
   - Reflection losses
   - Number of bounces
   
   Measured in Pascals or dB re 1 μPa.

3. PHASE:
   Position in wave cycle when ray arrives.
   
   Used for:
   - Interferometry
   - Phase-difference localization
   - Coherent processing

4. FREQUENCY:
   Center frequency (or frequency range for chirp).
   
   Different frequencies travel different paths due to:
   - Different absorption
   - Different scattering
   - Refraction effects

5. DIRECTION:
   Ray arrival direction (if hydrophone is directional).
   
   Used for:
   - Bearing estimation
   - Beamforming
   - Spatial filtering

MULTIPLE ARRIVALS:
------------------
A hydrophone typically receives MANY rays:
  - Direct path (first, strongest usually)
  - Reflected paths (later, weaker)
  - Multiple-bounce paths (latest, weakest)

Example output for one emitter:
  Hydrophone-1: 47 arrivals
    First: 35.2 ms, amplitude 0.156
    Last: 187.5 ms, amplitude 0.0008

ARRIVAL TIME DISTRIBUTION:
  Most arrivals cluster in first 50-100 ms
  Long tail extends to 200+ ms (many bounces)

SIGNAL PROCESSING:
------------------
Real hydrophones would process arrivals to:
  1. Matched filtering (for chirp signals)
  2. Time-of-arrival estimation
  3. Beamforming (if array)
  4. Doppler compensation (if moving)
  5. Noise filtering

10. PRACTICAL EXAMPLES
================================================================================

EXAMPLE 1: DIRECT PATH DETECTION
---------------------------------
Scenario:
  - Emitter: (10, 20, 2m), frequency 70 kHz, power 175 dB
  - Hydrophone: (50, 60, 10m)
  - Direct path distance: 65.6m

Calculations:

1. Travel time:
   t = 65.6m / 1500 m/s = 43.7 ms

2. Geometric spreading loss:
   TL_spread = 20×log₁₀(65.6/1) = 36.3 dB

3. Absorption loss (70 kHz, 3.5 dB/km):
   TL_abs = 3.5 × 0.0656 = 0.23 dB

4. Scattering loss (negligible):
   TL_scatter ≈ 0.0 dB

5. Total loss:
   TL_total = 36.3 + 0.23 = 36.5 dB

6. Received level:
   RL = SL - TL = 175 - 36.5 = 138.5 dB

7. Amplitude (relative to source):
   A/A₀ = 10^(-36.5/20) = 0.015 = 1.5%


EXAMPLE 2: SINGLE REFLECTION PATH
----------------------------------
Scenario:
  - Same emitter and hydrophone
  - Ray bounces off bottom (concrete, R=0.90) at (30, 40, 50m)
  - Path: 38m + bounce + 42m = 80m total
  - Incidence angle: 35°

Calculations:

1. Travel time:
   t = 80m / 1500 m/s = 53.3 ms

2. Geometric spreading:
   TL_spread = 20×log₁₀(80/1) = 38.1 dB

3. Absorption:
   TL_abs = 3.5 × 0.080 = 0.28 dB

4. Reflection loss (R=0.90 at 35°):
   R_eff ≈ 0.88 (angle-dependent)
   TL_refl = -20×log₁₀(0.88) = 1.1 dB

5. Total loss:
   TL_total = 38.1 + 0.28 + 1.1 = 39.5 dB

6. Received level:
   RL = 175 - 39.5 = 135.5 dB

7. Amplitude:
   A/A₀ = 0.011 = 1.1%

COMPARISON: Direct vs Reflected
  - Direct: 43.7ms, 138.5 dB
  - Reflected: 53.3ms, 135.5 dB
  
  Time difference: 9.6ms (can be resolved)
  Amplitude difference: 3 dB (reflected weaker)


EXAMPLE 3: HIGHLY REVERBERANT PATH (5 BOUNCES)
-----------------------------------------------
Scenario:
  - Emitter to hydrophone via 5 reflections
  - Total path: 165m
  - Each reflection: R ≈ 0.88 average
  - Mixed surface angles

Calculations:

1. Travel time:
   t = 165m / 1500 m/s = 110 ms

2. Geometric spreading:
   TL_spread = 20×log₁₀(165/1) = 44.3 dB

3. Absorption:
   TL_abs = 3.5 × 0.165 = 0.58 dB

4. Five reflections:
   R_total = 0.88^5 = 0.528
   TL_refl = -20×log₁₀(0.528) = 5.5 dB

5. Total loss:
   TL_total = 44.3 + 0.58 + 5.5 = 50.4 dB

6. Received level:
   RL = 175 - 50.4 = 124.6 dB

7. Amplitude:
   A/A₀ = 0.003 = 0.3%

OBSERVATION: After 5 bounces, signal is ~30× weaker than direct path!

================================================================================
SUMMARY OF KEY PHYSICS
================================================================================

1. SOUND PROPAGATION:
   ✓ Pressure waves in water
   ✓ Speed ~1500 m/s (depends on T, S, P)
   ✓ Frequency range: 30-200 kHz

2. RAY APPROXIMATION:
   ✓ Valid for high frequency (λ << object size)
   ✓ Straight lines until reflection/refraction
   ✓ Energy carried along ray paths

3. ENERGY LOSSES:
   ✓ Spreading: -20 dB per 10× distance increase
   ✓ Absorption: Frequency-dependent (Thorp equation)
   ✓ Scattering: Particles/bubbles (minor effect)
   ✓ Reflections: Material + angle dependent

4. REFLECTIONS:
   ✓ Law of reflection (θᵢ = θᵣ)
   ✓ Energy loss each bounce (1-R)
   ✓ Phase inversion at surface
   ✓ Angle-dependent losses

5. MULTIPATH:
   ✓ Many paths from source to receiver
   ✓ Different times, amplitudes, phases
   ✓ Causes interference and dispersion

6. DETECTION:
   ✓ Hydrophones record time, amplitude, phase
   ✓ Multiple arrivals per emitter
   ✓ First arrival often strongest (direct path)

7. FREQUENCY EFFECTS:
   ✓ Low freq: Long range, poor resolution
   ✓ High freq: Short range, good resolution
   ✓ Tradeoff in system design
